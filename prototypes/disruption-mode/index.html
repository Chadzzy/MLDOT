Sun Airlines · Disruption Mode
Concept walkthrough · Four stages

The flight broke. The journey shouldn't.

Disruption Mode is what the passenger sees when the network has already moved on without them. It assumes the recovery has already happened — the airline's job on the glass is not to ask the passenger to solve it, but to show them it is solved, and where to walk.

Every number, rule and threshold on these screens is taken from frank-morales2020/MLxDL — principally DEEPSEEK_RECOVERAI_AGENT_PAXDEMO.ipynb, whose RAI_PROFILE defines the carrier (Sun Airlines), its fleet, its home bases and its minimum connection times. The passenger-facing layer is the new work; the operating logic underneath is the notebook's.

The argument

Disruption Mode is a posture, not a screen

Solve, then tell

The recovery agent runs before the passenger is notified. protect_pnr means the seat exists by the time the phone buzzes — so the message is "you're covered", never "please rebook".

Show the rule, not just the result

23 min is meaningless alone. Against a stated 30 min minimum it becomes a decision she can understand and argue with.

Rank in the open

Own metal, then reserve, then interline — the agent's real search order, shown as the passenger's real menu. The ranking is the airline's economics; hiding it is what breeds distrust.

End at the belt

A journey isn't recovered until the bag, the transfer and the money are settled. Most apps stop at the new boarding pass.
