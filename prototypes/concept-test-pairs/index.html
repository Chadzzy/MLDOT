Concept test · Stimulus sheet DISRUPTION UI · 4 PAIRS · 8 SCREENS

Same information. Opposite instincts.

Four disruption scenarios, each built twice. In every pair the underlying facts are identical — the same delay, the same connection, the same rebooking. What changes is a single deliberate contradiction in how the airline chooses to behave.

Show A and B to different participants, never the same one. The variable under test is named on each band, and the thing to measure is stated at the foot of it. Screens are drawn in the current Cathay Pacific visual language so that participants react to the behaviour, not to unfamiliar styling.

Whether early-and-unsure buys trust or manufactures dread. A gives her 36 minutes of knowing-but-not-knowing; B gives her 36 minutes of not knowing there was anything to know. Whether one answer reads as care or as control. The tell in A is the tap rate on "See other options" — high tap-through means the single answer is landing as withholding, not service. Watch for

Whether admitting the alternative reads as honesty or as a downgrade. B is the riskier screen: it tells her something faster exists, which can convert a solved problem back into an open decision. Measure perceived effort — "how hard do you think Cathay worked on this?" — against satisfaction with the outcome. The failure mode to look for is the two diverging: effort perceived as higher, satisfaction lower. If that happens, the trade-off is better placed one layer down, on request, rather than on the confirmation itself.

How to run these

Between-subjects, never within. A participant who sees A and then B is no longer reacting to either — they are reacting to the comparison, which no real passenger ever gets.

Hold the facts constant

Every pair carries identical underlying facts: same flight numbers, same times, same seat, same hotel. If a participant's preference tracks a fact rather than the behaviour, the pair is broken.

Scenario 02 is asymmetric

A and B are shown at different clock times by design — 17:50 versus 18:26. The 36-minute gap is the variable. Do not equalise it.
